P/01 How much rain falls between the gauges?Daily rainfall at ungauged locations in India, for crop insurance. +11.4% vs kriging, locked test1 in 20 gauges ≈ all-gauge kriging 2026

Crop-insurance payouts in India are triggered by rainfall at village-cluster level, but most villages have no gauge. I estimate daily rainfall where there's no station and test it the hard way: whole stations held out, spatial-block cross-validation, and one-shot locked tests on years the model never saw.

The test bed is about 3,300 Indian rain gauges active from 1955 to 1970, dense enough to hide 95% of them and score against the truth. ERA5 is the fair gridded baseline for that era, because gauge-based products ingest the test gauges.

  • Gauge-only ML ties kriging to within ~1% (trained on 438k US and Australia examples). The gain comes from ERA5 physics (CAPE, column water vapour, moisture divergence, winds) plus terrain priors such as upwind barriers and coast distance.
  • A second blind year (1959) gave +13.1%. In the satellite era, a learned merge beats raw IMERG Late by 1–5%.
Open problem. Every method I've tested, mine included, catches at most about a third of days with 64.5 mm or more of rain and under-predicts them. Those are the days that trigger payouts and floods. Technical note and code due December 2026.

P/02 rlvrexamplesA review and atlas of RLVR environments, and which could become companies. 54 ecosystems · 828 tasks213 verifiers checked 2026

A review of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), where a program checks the model's answer instead of a judge model. It asks two questions: which RLVR environments haven't been built yet, and could any of them become a company?

With a team of research agents, I mapped 54 environment ecosystems, 828 named tasks from the major gyms and 213 open-source verifiers, flagging the ones never used as an LLM reward. The result is a catalog of candidate environments. Each entry gives the reward check, difficulty knobs, a novelty rating against the closest prior work, and a startup tier.

Two threads stood out: verified finance-ops agents, and India's GST e-invoice → e-way bill → input-tax-credit chain.

P/03 FleetA merge train for many cheap coding agents under one planner. 1,051–1,878 merges/hour under chaos10/10 chaos runs, 0 violations 2026

Fleet runs many cheap coding agents in parallel under a planning model, with a ticket board, fenced leases and a merge train. A chaos bench randomly kills workers and the merger mid-flight to prove nothing gets lost or merged twice.

The release candidate passed 10 of 10 chaos runs with zero invariant violations across 152 tests. It sustained 1,051–1,878 merges an hour while being sabotaged, roughly 3–4× my first version (about 400 an hour).
