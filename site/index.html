Climate ML · India

How much rain falls between the gauges?

Crop-insurance payouts in India are triggered by rainfall at village-cluster level, but most villages have no gauge. I'm estimating daily rainfall at locations with no station, and testing it the hard way: whole stations held out, spatial-block cross-validation, and one-shot locked tests on years the model never saw.

The test bed is about 3,300 Indian rain gauges active from 1955 to 1970, a dense historical network that lets me hide 95% of stations and score against the truth. ERA5 is the fair gridded baseline for that era, because gauge-based products ingest the test gauges.

+11.4%RMSE gain over kriging on a locked 1967 test at 5% gauge density (95% CI 9.4–13.3)
1 in 20gauges plus ERA5 physics matches kriging that uses every gauge, in two blind years
~20%less simulated payout error than the nearest-station rule in a grape weather-index insurance study
  • Gauge-only ML ties kriging to within ~1% (trained on 438k US and Australia examples). The gain comes from ERA5 fields (CAPE, column water vapour, moisture divergence, winds) plus terrain priors such as upwind barriers and coast distance.
  • A second blind year (1959) gave +13.1%. In the satellite era, a learned merge beats raw IMERG Late (near real time) by 1–5%.
  • The insurance study covers 180 gauges and 16 seasons under Maharashtra's grape weather-index scheme.
The open problem. Every method I've tested, mine included, catches at most about a third of days with 64.5 mm or more of rain and under-predicts them. Those are the days that trigger insurance payouts and floods. That's what I'm working on now, and a technical note with code is due December 2026.

I'd especially like to talk to people working on precipitation estimation, gauge–satellite merging, forecast verification for extremes, and parametric or crop insurance in India.

RL · Research review

rlvrexamples: an atlas of RLVR environments

A review of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), where a program checks the model's answer instead of a judge model. It asks two questions: which RLVR environments haven't been built yet, and could any of them become a company?

With a team of research agents, I mapped 54 environment ecosystems, 828 named tasks from the major gyms and 213 open-source verifiers, flagging the ones never used as an LLM reward. The result is a catalog of candidate environments. Each entry gives the reward check, difficulty knobs, a novelty rating against the closest prior work, and a startup tier.

Two threads stood out: verified finance-ops agents, and India's GST e-invoice → e-way bill → input-tax-credit chain.

Agents · Distributed systems

Fleet: a merge train for parallel coding agents

Fleet runs many cheap coding agents in parallel under a planning model, with a ticket board, fenced leases and a merge train. A chaos bench randomly kills workers and the merger mid-flight to prove nothing gets lost or merged twice.

The release candidate passed 10 of 10 chaos runs with zero invariant violations across 152 tests. It sustained 1,051–1,878 merges an hour while being sabotaged, roughly 3–4× my first version (about 400 an hour).
